Methodology

1. The universal bet

Flashcat bets $100 flat on every event. No Kelly, no fractional sizing, no abstention. The research question is: can a probability blend, scored across many sources, beat the closing market on $100-flat? Position sizing is a Phase 2 problem.

2. Probability blending

For each event, we collect a home-win probability from every source that has one:

  • The Odds API — devigged consensus across US books
  • ESPN scoreboard / predictor — ESPN's gameProjection field
  • Polymarket — last-trade price on the relevant market
  • Market consensus — devigged moneyline from book-average closing odds
  • (Phase 2: Pinnacle, DraftKings, FanDuel, Kalshi, FiveThirtyEight, Massey/Sagarin, KenPom, BPI — stubs are in the codebase)

Each source has a weight w_s ∈ [0, 1] with Σw_s = 1. The blended home probability is:

p_blend(home) = Σ_s ( w_s × p_s(home) )

On a fresh deploy, weights are uniform across active sources. After each backtest, weights update to favor sources with better calibration.

3. Pick rule

We bet $100 on whichever side has the higher blended probability. The only exception is the tie-breaker band: when p_blend(home) ∈ [0.48, 0.52], we bet the underdog by moneyline. This encodes the "favorite is a sucker's bet" hunch — in coin-flip games, the market premium on the slight favorite has historically been overpriced.

4. Source reweighting

After every backtest, each source gets a Brier score B_s = mean((p_s − y)²), where y ∈ {0, 1} is the home-win outcome. New weights are a tempered softmax over negative Brier (so low-Brier sources get more weight):

w_s = softmax( −τ × B_s )    where τ = 4.0 by default

Sources with fewer than 20 graded events are excluded from reweighting — they keep their prior weight until the sample size is meaningful. Snapshots of every weight update are saved to SQLite (weight_history table) so we can audit drift.

5. The two signal flags

5a. Chalk-overpriced (favorite-longshot bias)

For each event we compute the devigged implied probability of the favorite from book averages. If that implied probability is more than 5 percentage points above the model's blended probability for the favorite, we flag chalk-overpriced. This is the modern restatement of the classic favorite-longshot bias result.

5b. Reverse line movement + cross-book dispersion

For events where we have both opening and current moneylines:

  • RLM: if the home implied probability moved ≥ 2 percentage points away from opening, we flag the direction of the move (reverse-line-movement-toward-home or toward-away). Sharp money pushes lines against the public bet distribution.
  • Book dispersion: if the spread between the highest and lowest implied probabilities on the underdog side across books exceeds 4 percentage points, we flag book-dispersion-dog. A wide spread means books disagree, and the soft books are exploitable.

6. Backtest

Phase 1 backtests on the most recent complete NFL season via nfl_data_py. Each historical event is simulated as a $100 flat bet at the book-average closing line; outcomes determine P&L. The bankroll curve, calibration bins, and ROI slices (overall / chalk-only / dog-only / RLM-only / dispersion-only) are recomputed every run.

7. What is out of scope (Phase 1)

  • Live in-game probabilities
  • Real-money execution
  • Account / bankroll / Kelly sizing
  • Sport-specific deep models (those are Phase 2 — Flashcat is the blender, not the sport model)
  • Scraping behind login walls
